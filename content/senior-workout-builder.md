## Why Senior Strength Training Matters

Muscle loss (sarcopenia) accelerates after age 60 — we can lose 3–8% of muscle mass per decade, with rates increasing after 70. The consequences show up as weakness, slowed walking speed, increased fall risk, and reduced ability to handle daily tasks.

The good news: **strength training reverses this.** Even beginning a resistance program in your 70s or 80s produces meaningful improvements in muscle size and function within 8–12 weeks.

### The Five Components of a Complete Senior Workout

The builder structures every session around five categories because each serves a different function:

1. **Lower Body** — Squats, step-ups, and leg presses preserve the quad and glute strength needed to rise from chairs, climb stairs, and prevent falls.
2. **Upper Body** — Rows, curls, and presses maintain the strength for lifting groceries, reaching overhead, and pushing yourself up from low surfaces.
3. **Core** — Bracing, pelvic tilts, and bird dogs build the stability that protects your spine and keeps you upright.
4. **Balance** — Single-leg stands and weight shifts directly train the neural pathways that prevent falls — the most important safety work you can do.
5. **Flexibility** — Stretches counteract the tightening that comes with age and inactivity, keeping joints mobile and reducing injury risk.

## Getting Started: What to Expect

**Week 1–2:** Focus on form over intensity. Movements may feel awkward — that's normal. Use the lightest resistance and take extra rest between sets.

**Week 3–4:** Exercises start to feel more natural. You may notice it's easier to get up from chairs or climb stairs — those are real functional gains.

**Month 2+:** Begin gradually increasing resistance or reps. The builder's "Generate New Workout" button shuffles exercises within your filters to keep sessions varied and prevent adaptation plateaus.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How many days per week should I strength train?**
Two to three non-consecutive days is the standard recommendation for older adults — giving muscles 48 hours to recover between sessions.

**Is it safe to exercise with arthritis?**
Usually yes, and often beneficial. Low-impact resistance training can actually reduce arthritis pain by strengthening the muscles around affected joints. Always consult your doctor for specific guidance.

**What if I feel sore the day after?**
Mild muscle soreness (DOMS) 24–48 hours after exercise is normal and a sign the muscles are adapting. Sharp pain during exercise, joint pain, or chest discomfort are reasons to stop immediately and consult your physician.
