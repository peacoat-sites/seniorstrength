# How Senior Strength Is Written

Senior Strength covers strength, balance and mobility training for adults over 60, along with the nutrition that helps keep muscle with age. This page explains, plainly, how the articles are made.

## How articles are made

Articles on Senior Strength are drafted with AI writing tools. Each draft starts from public reference sources on the topic, including those listed below, and from recent news when a topic is changing.

Before an article is published, a second automated pass reviews it and removes or rewrites anything it cannot stand behind: statistics, prices or dates it is not confident are correct, studies or organizations that may be misattributed, and personal stories presented as if they happened to the writer.

Articles are not reviewed by a doctor or physical therapist before publication. Use them as background for a conversation with one, not as a substitute.

The site owner audits published articles in batches, merging pages that overlap, deleting weak ones and correcting errors.

## Who writes here

The bylines on Senior Strength (Patricia Williams, Helen Santos, James Cooper, Linda Chen, Robert Davis) are pen names, one for each area of coverage. They are not real individuals, they hold no professional licenses, and their profile images are illustrations, not photographs of real people.

## Sources we rely on

- [National Institute on Aging](https://www.nia.nih.gov/): Exercise and healthy aging
- [CDC: Physical Activity](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity/): Older-adult activity guidance
- [National Institutes of Health (NIH)](https://www.nih.gov/): Health research
- [American College of Sports Medicine](https://www.acsm.org/): Exercise science

Where an article depends on a specific figure, rule or study, it links to or names the source so you can check it.

## Limits of this site

This is general exercise information, not medical advice. Check with your doctor before starting a new program if you have heart disease, high blood pressure that is not under control, diabetes, osteoporosis, a recent fall, surgery or joint replacement, or symptoms such as chest pain or dizziness when you exert yourself. Stop and get medical help right away if you have chest pain, severe shortness of breath or sudden dizziness during exercise.

## Corrections

If you find an error, please report it through the [contact page](/contact/). Reports go directly to the site owner, and confirmed errors are corrected in the article.

## How the site makes money

Senior Strength may earn money from display advertising and from affiliate links, including Amazon links (as an Amazon Associate, the site earns from qualifying purchases). Advertisers and affiliate partners do not see or approve articles before they are published.
